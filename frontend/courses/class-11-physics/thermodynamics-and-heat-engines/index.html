Ideal Gas State Equation

When you compress gas in a bicycle pump rapidly, the pump barrel warms up. You performed mechanical work on the gas, elevating its internal energy without supplying heat.

The First Law of Thermodynamics captures this bookkeeping: Heat added = Increase in internal energy + Work done by expansion.

First Law of Thermodynamics & Ideal Gas Law

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW · P · V = n · R · T
ΔQHeat Exchanged

Thermal energy transferred across system boundary (+ when absorbed, - when released). (SI: Joules (J))

ΔUInternal Energy Change

Change in total kinetic energy of chaotic molecular motion (ΔU = n C_v ΔT). (SI: Joules (J))

ΔWThermodynamic Work

Mechanical work performed by expanding gas (ΔW = P · ΔV). (SI: Joules (J))

P, V, TState Variables

Pressure (Pa), Volume (m³), and absolute Temperature (Kelvin). (SI: Pa, m³, K)

Thermodynamic Transformations: Isothermal vs Adiabatic Processes

Aspect Isothermal Process Adiabatic Process
Constrained Condition Temperature remains strictly constant (ΔT = 0, ΔU = 0) Zero heat exchange with surroundings (ΔQ = 0)
First Law Balance All heat absorbed turns into work: ΔQ = ΔW Work is done entirely at cost of internal energy: ΔW = -ΔU
Governing Equation P · V = Constant (Boyle's Law curve) P · V^γ = Constant (steeper curve, γ = C_p / C_v)
Physical Execution Very slow process conducted in highly thermally conductive container Very rapid process conducted in heavily insulated container

Step-by-Step Derivation: Work Done in an Isothermal Gas Expansion

Step 1: Infinitesimal Work Definition

When a gas expands against an external pressure P by small volume dV, the work done is: dW = P · dV.

Step 2: Express Pressure from Ideal Gas Law

Since P·V = n·R·T, express pressure as a function of volume: P(V) = (n·R·T) / V.

Step 3: Integrate from Initial Volume V₁ to Final Volume V₂

W = ∫ [V₁ to V₂] P dV = ∫ [V₁ to V₂] (n·R·T / V) dV.

Step 4: Factor Out Constant Temperature T

Since temperature is constant in an isothermal process: W = n·R·T ∫ [V₁ to V₂] (1 / V) dV = n·R·T [ln(V)] [V₁ to V₂].

Step 5: Final Isothermal Work Expression

W = n · R · T · ln(V₂ / V₁) = 2.303 · n · R · T · log₁₀(V₂ / V₁). When V₂ > V₁, W is positive (gas does work).

Worked Example

An ideal gas system absorbs 500 J of heat from its surroundings. If the gas expands and performs 200 J of work against a piston, calculate the change in its internal energy.

  1. Identify First Law of Thermodynamics ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW, where ΔQ = +500 J (heat added) and ΔW = +200 J (work done by gas).
  2. Rearrange for Change in Internal Energy ΔU ΔU = ΔQ - ΔW = 500 J - 200 J = +300 J.
  3. Physical Interpretation The internal energy of the gas increased by 300 J, which elevates its temperature.

Answer: ΔU = +300 J (internal energy increases by 300 Joules).
