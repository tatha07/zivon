Sound Waves & Propagation

Learning Objectives:

  • Differentiate clearly between longitudinal (sound) and transverse waves.
  • Calculate wave speed, wavelength, and frequency using v = f·λ.
  • Demonstrate wave superposition and interference (constructive vs destructive).
  • Explain how amplitude determines loudness and frequency determines pitch.

Key Physics Formulas

v = f * λ

Wave speed formula

T = 1 / f

Time period definition

Sound in air is a longitudinal mechanical wave: a vibrating tuning fork pushes adjacent air molecules together into 'compressions' (high pressure) and pulls back creating 'rarefactions' (low pressure).

Light and electromagnetic waves, by contrast, are transverse: electric and magnetic fields oscillate perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

Wave Equation & Frequency Relationships

v = f · λ · T = 1 / f
vWave Propagation Speed

Speed at which energy and phase advance through the medium. (SI: Meters per second (m/s))

fWave Frequency

Number of full oscillation cycles passing a fixed point per unit time. (SI: Hertz (Hz = 1/s))

λWavelength

Distance between consecutive crests, compressions, or in-phase points. (SI: Meters (m))

TTime Period

Duration required to execute one complete particle oscillation. (SI: Seconds (s))

Wave Categorization: Transverse vs Longitudinal Waves

Aspect Transverse Waves Longitudinal Waves
Particle Oscillation Perpendicular (90°) to direction of wave propagation Parallel (0° / 180°) along line of wave propagation
Anatomy Alternating crests (peaks) and troughs (valleys) Alternating compressions (high density) and rarefactions
Polarization Can be plane-polarized (light, EM waves) Cannot be polarized (pressure fluctuations)
Typical Examples Light waves, radio waves, ripples on water surface Sound waves in air, ultrasound, seismic P-waves

Step-by-Step Derivation: The Universal Wave Speed Equation v = f · λ

Step 1: General Definition of Speed

Speed is distance traversed divided by elapsed time: Speed = Distance / Time.

Step 2: Relate Wavelength to Wave Period

In exactly one period (T seconds), a wave profile advances forward by one complete wavelength (λ meters). Thus: v = λ / T.

Step 3: Relate Period to Frequency

Frequency f is the inverse of time period: f = 1 / T &rArr; 1 / T = f.

Step 4: Combine Expressions

Substituting 1/T into the velocity relation yields: v = f · λ. This relation universally applies to all mechanical, sound, and electromagnetic waves.

Worked Example

A sound wave has a frequency of 2 kHz (2000 Hz) and wavelength of 17 cm. How long will it take to travel 1.7 km?

  1. Find Wave Speed v = f · λ f = 2000 Hz, λ = 17 cm = 0.17 m. Speed v = 2000 × 0.17 = 340 m/s.
  2. Compute Time to Cover Distance Distance d = 1.7 km = 1700 m. Time t = d / v = 1700 m / 340 m/s = 5.0 seconds.

Answer: The sound wave travels at 340 m/s and takes 5.0 seconds to cover 1.7 km.

If a wave's frequency is doubled while traveling through the same uniform medium, what happens to its wavelength?

Why?

In a uniform medium, speed v is constant. Since v = f·λ, doubling frequency f requires wavelength λ to halve.
