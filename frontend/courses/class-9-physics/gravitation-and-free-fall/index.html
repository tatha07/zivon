Gravitation & Free Fall

Learning Objectives:

  • Explain what unbalanced force does and calculate resultant net force vectors.
  • Apply Newton's Second Law (F = ma) to solve numerical acceleration and displacement problems.
  • Draw and interpret free body diagrams illustrating applied, friction, and normal forces.
  • Understand why action-reaction pairs never cancel each other out.

Key Physics Formulas

F_net = m · a

F in Newtons (N), m in kilograms (kg), a in m/s²

f_k = μ_k · N

Kinetic friction equals coefficient of kinetic friction times normal force

Imagine pushing a 10 kg box resting on a concrete floor. If you push lightly with 4 N, the box does not budge because static friction pushes back with 4 N. The forces are balanced.

As soon as your applied force exceeds maximum static friction, the box breaks free. The difference between your push and kinetic friction is the net unbalanced force that accelerates the box.

Worked Example

A student pushes a stationary 20 kg crate across a smooth lab floor with a constant force of 50 N. If a constant friction force of 10 N opposes the motion, calculate: (a) the net force, (b) the acceleration of the crate, and (c) its velocity after 3 seconds.

  1. Find the net force F_net = Applied Force - Friction Force = 50 N - 10 N = 40 N (directed forward).
  2. Calculate acceleration using F = ma a = F_net / m = 40 N / 20 kg = 2.0 m/s².
  3. Compute velocity after 3 seconds Using first equation of kinematics v = u + at, where initial velocity u = 0: v = 0 + (2.0 m/s²)(3 s) = 6.0 m/s.

Answer: Net force is 40 N forward; acceleration is 2.0 m/s²; final velocity is 6.0 m/s.

Derivation: Newton's Second Law to Kinematic Displacement

Step 1: Newton's Law Definition

F_net = d(p)/dt = m · (dv/dt) = m · a

Step 2: Relate to Acceleration

a = F_net / m

Step 3: Integrate with Respect to Time

Starting from rest (u=0), velocity v(t) = a·t = (F_net / m)·t

Step 4: Total Displacement

s = 1/2 · a · t² = 1/2 · (F_net / m) · t²

A spacecraft in deep outer space is cruising with engines turned off. If no gravitational or friction forces act on it, what happens to its speed?

Why?

According to Newton's First Law, an object in motion continues with constant velocity unless an external unbalanced force acts upon it.

Common Student Misconceptions

  • MYTHA continuous force is required to keep an object moving at constant speed.
    REALITYAccording to Newton's first law, once moving, an object requires zero net force to maintain constant velocity; friction is what usually slows it down.

Glossary of Terms

Force
A push or pull possessing both magnitude and direction, measured in Newtons (N).
Net Force
The resultant single vector obtained by combining all concurrent forces on a body.
Acceleration
The rate of change of velocity with respect to time (m/s²).
