When you look through a magnifying glass or camera lens, light rays refract at spherical glass surfaces. Depending on where the object stands relative to focal point F and center 2F, the image shifts dramatically.

Thin Lens Equation & Optical Magnification

1/f = 1/v - 1/u · m = v/u
fFocal Length

Distance from optical center O to principal focus F (+ for convex, - for concave). (SI: cm / meters)

uObject Distance

Distance from optical center to object (- by Cartesian sign convention). (SI: cm / meters)

vImage Distance

Distance from optical center to image (+ for real image, - for virtual). (SI: cm / meters)

mLinear Magnification

Ratio of image height to object height (negative implies inverted). (SI: Dimensionless)

Spherical Lenses: Convex vs Concave Lenses

Aspect Convex (Converging) Lens Concave (Diverging) Lens
Physical Shape Thicker in the middle, thinner at edges Thinner in the middle, thicker at edges
Action on Rays Converges parallel incident light rays to real focus Diverges parallel rays away from virtual focus
Focal Length Sign Positive (+f) Negative (-f)
Image Formation Forms both real inverted and virtual erect images Always forms diminished, erect, virtual images
Common Applications Magnifying glasses, cameras, projectors, hypermetropia Peepholes in doors, flashlights, myopia eyeglasses

Step-by-Step Geometric Derivation of the Thin Lens Formula

Step 1: Identify Similar Triangles with Optical Center

Consider object AB and inverted image A'B' with optical center O. Right triangles ΔABO and ΔA'B'O are similar: A'B' / AB = OB' / OB = v / (-u) = -v/u.

Step 2: Trace the Ray Parallel to Axis Through Focus

Ray AD parallel to axis refracts through F₂. Triangles ΔODF₂ and ΔA'B'F₂ are similar (where OD = AB): A'B' / OD = A'B' / AB = B'F₂ / OF₂.

Step 3: Express Distance B'F₂

B'F₂ = OB' - OF₂ = v - f. Thus: A'B' / AB = (v - f) / f.

Step 4: Equate Both Ratio Expressions

v / (-u) = (v - f) / f &rArr; -v/u = v/f - 1.

Step 5: Divide Both Sides by Image Distance v

-1/u = 1/f - 1/v &rArr; 1/f = 1/v - 1/u. This is the celebrated Gaussian thin lens formula.

Worked Example

A convex lens has a focal length of 15 cm. At what distance should an object 4 cm tall be placed from the optical center so that a real, inverted image is formed at a distance of 30 cm on the other side? Also calculate the image height.

  1. Identify Given Values with Signs f = +15 cm (convex lens), v = +30 cm (real image on other side), h_o = 4 cm. We need u and h_i.
  2. Apply Lens Formula 1/f = 1/v - 1/u 1/u = 1/v - 1/f = 1/30 - 1/15 = 1/30 - 2/30 = -1/30. Therefore, u = -30 cm.
  3. Compute Magnification m = v / u = h_i / h_o m = (+30 cm) / (-30 cm) = -1.0. Thus, h_i = m · h_o = -1.0 · 4 cm = -4 cm.

Answer: The object must be placed at 30 cm in front of the lens (u = -30 cm, at 2F). The image is 4 cm tall and inverted.

Where should an object be placed in front of a convex lens to get a real image of the same size as the object?

Why?

When placed at 2F, 1/v = 1/f - 1/(2f) = 1/(2f), so v = 2F. Magnification m = -v/u = -2F/2F = -1, meaning identical size.

Common Student Misconceptions

  • MYTHRays bend towards the medium surface during refraction.
    REALITYRays bend towards the NORMAL when entering an optically denser medium, not towards the interface.

Glossary of Terms

Focal Length (f)
Distance between optical centre O and principal focus F of the lens.
Real Image
An image formed by the actual intersection of light rays, which can be captured on a screen.
Virtual Image
An image formed where rays only appear to diverge from, which cannot be captured on a screen.
